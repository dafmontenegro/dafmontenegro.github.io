## How he thinks and what he values


### Tit For Tat, his life philosophy

- **Tit For Tat** is, for him, a life philosophy. The strategy was introduced by Anatol Rapoport in the competition Robert Axelrod organized in 1980, and it is the emblematic winning strategy of the Prisoner's Dilemma, one of his favorite problems in game theory (he recommends Veritasium's video on it). It has two rules: (1) start by cooperating, as a gesture of goodwill toward the other; (2) mirror the opponent's last move, whether they cooperated or not. Axelrod identified four qualities behind the strategies that succeeded, and Daniel Felipe tries to live by them day by day: nice (have good will and do not take advantage of your opponent), forgiving (cooperate when necessary, even if you have been let down before), retaliatory (if your opponent defects, strike back immediately; don't be a pushover) and clear (be consistent with your strategy and actions). For him real life is not a zero-sum game, where one must lose for the other to win; his words, in English: "I hold on to the hope that where we all win, we also win ourselves." (In Spanish, paraphrase it without quotation marks: conserva la esperanza de que donde todos ganamos, también nos ganamos a nosotros mismos.)
